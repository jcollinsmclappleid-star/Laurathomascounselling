**Standfirst:** In-Touch is my messaging-led support for parents who want a continuing conversation about the situations arising in family life.

### When another question comes up

A difficult conversation does not always happen just before an appointment. You might want to reflect on what happened, think through a response or return to something we have already discussed. In-Touch is designed around that need for continuing parent guidance.

### Understand the arrangement before you begin

We start by discussing your family situation and whether this way of working is suitable. The agreed arrangement sets out how contact works, what is included and when replies are available.

Voice or text messages provide a way to bring a situation into the conversation. Guidance relates to the context you share; this is a relationship with a practitioner, not an automated advice tool.

### Clear boundaries matter

In-Touch is not an emergency or continuously monitored service. Working days, response windows and any session allowance need to be agreed before support begins. There is no promise of an immediate reply.

If your main need is your own therapeutic space or counselling for a young person, those services have separate explanations and enquiry routes.

You may come across ideas from RESET, a relevant resource or one of my books. They support the conversation rather than replacing an understanding of your particular circumstances.

### Find out whether it fits

An introductory conversation can cover your questions, the current format and the practical terms. Current options will be explained before you commit. If In-Touch is not suitable, we can discuss a more relevant next step.

## R04 · P1 · A more considered response, with RESET

New counselling URL: `/parenting/responding-with-reset/`

Navigation: The RESET approach

**Standfirst:** RESET is the framework I use to organise a more thoughtful response when family interactions feel difficult. Here is an introduction to its purpose and the ideas behind it.

### A structure for the conversation

When there is a lot happening at once, it can be hard to decide what deserves your attention. RESET brings together noticing the situation, understanding emotion, communicating what matters and considering a next step. It is an approach to explore in context, rather than a script that fits every family.

### Recognise the issue

Begin by considering what may be happening in the situation, rather than relying only on the behaviour you first notice.

### Empathise in the moment

The framework makes room for the teenager’s perspective and the experience behind the interaction.

### Say what matters

Consider the important message, value or boundary, and how it can be expressed clearly.

### Emotionally regulate together

This part of the approach focuses on emotional steadiness within the interaction.

### Take action with confidence

The final step concerns a considered response to the situation, informed by what came before.

### Explore it with the right context

The framework does not replace individual assessment or professional support when that is needed. My parenting services, resources and books offer ways to explore related ideas in context.
